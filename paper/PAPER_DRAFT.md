# Hou's Navier-Stokes singularity scenario: a resolved front in the class excluded by the Liouville theorems, and its certified centrifugal instability

*Draft 0.7, 2026-09-07 (title per Andrew's choice). All numbers final at this stage. Author list, affiliations and acknowledgements to be added.*

## Abstract

Hou's interior scenario for singularity formation in the three-dimensional axisymmetric Euler equations, and its Navier-Stokes continuation with a two-stage viscosity, are the most developed candidates for a finite-time singularity of a smooth finite-energy incompressible flow. We continue the viscous scenario at Hou's first-stage viscosity to 331 times the initial amplitude with a front-following axial map that resolves the sharp front to ten cells, and measure the quantity sup |u| |x'| that is the hypothesis of the Koch-Nadirashvili-Seregin-Sverak Liouville theorem for bounded ancient axisymmetric solutions with swirl. It does not involve the fitted singular time and is constant to 0.3 percent (72.2) from 144 to 331 times the initial amplitude, over 3.3 units of log(1/(T - t)), where the logarithmic growth of |u| r that Hou identified as the scenario's escape from the exclusion theorems would read thirty percent. The scenario as computed therefore lies in the class those theorems exclude. The front is the Rott-Lundgren strained layer under the measured collapse strain to a five percent profile residual, the amplitude is Type I (exponent 1.03 +- 0.03) with a converged singular time (0.0022865 +- 1e-7) by a log-derivative fit, the meridional Type I constant is bounded, and the scale-invariant local L^3 norm at the collapse scale decreases over four units of log(1/(T - t)). We also compute, for the first time, the linear stability of the interior collapse to non-axisymmetric perturbations, on the frozen and on the co-evolving base, in Euler and in Navier-Stokes at both of Hou's viscosities, with resolution certificates in both grid directions: the long-wave axisymmetric direction is marginally stable (peak 0.495 of the collapse rate at three resolutions), while the short-wave shearing continuum of the front is unstable relative to the collapse at every azimuthal wavenumber tested (m = 0, 1, 2), crossing half the collapse rate at 9 to 12 percent of the remaining time at viscosity 5e-4 and at 27 to 37 percent at 5e-3; the crossing fraction of the m = 1 family rises roughly logarithmically with viscosity from 5e-4 to 1e-2. Nonlinearly, every seed, state and viscosity tested depletes the collapse rather than transferring it. The instability is identified as the centrifugal instability of the front, whose circulation decreases outward, extended to every azimuthal wavenumber by the axial flow of the collapse: a one-dimensional parallel-flow model of the front column has an isolated unstable eigenvalue whose inviscid rate approaches the local Rayleigh bound (0.96 of the collapse rate) from below and whose viscous peak (0.81) matches the two-dimensional frozen-base rate (0.80), and that eigenvalue is certified by a Krawczyk enclosure with rigorous floating-point bounds for the collocation discretization of the model on an explicit rational polynomial field, at three resolutions agreeing to nine digits; the Leibovich-Stewartson sufficient condition is certified on the same kind of field by Sturm root counting in exact arithmetic. The local front structure is an exact infinite-energy Navier-Stokes solution, which explains why a core-resolving computation cannot observe the departure that finite energy forces at a few collapse lengths.

## 1. Introduction

The regularity problem for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations remains open in both directions. On the numerical side the strongest evidence for singular behaviour comes from the axisymmetric flows with swirl of Luo and Hou [LH14] at a solid boundary and, in the interior, of Hou [H23a] for Euler and Hou [H23b] for Navier-Stokes. The boundary scenario now has a computer-assisted proof of stable nearly self-similar blowup for Euler [CH25]; the interior scenario is supported by a self-similar profile fitted through amplifications of order 1e7 in Euler and, with a viscosity of 5e-4 raised to 5e-3 at a chosen time, in Navier-Stokes.

Two questions about these scenarios have not been asked in print. First, every stability statement about them, Hou's data-perturbation tests, the nonlinear stability of Chen and Hou, and the unstable-direction counts of the machine-learned profiles of Wang and coauthors, is a statement within the axisymmetric class; whether the collapsing state is stable to symmetry-breaking perturbations is unknown. Second, the viscous scenario's relation to the exclusion theorems for Type I singularities of axisymmetric flows with swirl [CSTY09, KNSS09, SS09] has been argued about but not measured: Hou identifies a logarithmic growth of r u^r as the mechanism by which his solution escapes those theorems [H24], and Barker [B25] has responded that the theorems' hypotheses do not involve that quantity.

This paper answers the first question and measures the quantities relevant to the second. Section 2 describes the codes and their validation. Section 3 gives the spectral table: the growth rates of azimuthal modes m = 0, 1, 2 about the collapsing state, in units of the collapse rate, with resolution certificates in both grid directions, together with the dependence of the m = 1 crossing on viscosity, the identification of the instability as the centrifugal instability of the front column, and a computer-assisted certificate of its discrete eigenvalue. Section 4 gives the nonlinear fate of the unstable perturbations. Section 5 gives the viscous continuation: the singular-time fit, the front-following map, and the identification of the front. Section 6 places the measured quantities against the theorems. Section 7 discusses what the results mean for constructions of Type II singularities and for the regularity side of the problem.

The results are negative in the sense the singularity literature cares about: the leading interior scenario is unstable to symmetry breaking, the instability depletes rather than intensifies the collapse, and the viscous continuation sits inside the class the theorems exclude. They are positive as characterization: the spectral table is the first non-axisymmetric stability computation of an axisymmetric blowup candidate, the front is identified as an exact local solution, and the theorem gap is stated with the measured constants attached.

## 2. Equations, codes and validation

### 2.1 Formulation

We use the Hou-Luo variables u1 = u_theta / r, omega1 = omega_theta / r, psi1 = psi / r on the periodic cylinder r <= 1, z in [0, 1) with the axisymmetric Navier-Stokes equations in the form of [H23a, H23b], the same initial data (the swirl profile 12000 (1 - r^2)^18 sin(2 pi z) / (1 + 12.5 sin^2(pi z))), and the same boundary condition at the wall. The base solver (axiphys) integrates these equations on a radial sinh-type map and an axial map, with the Hou-Li exponential filter, a moving radial map that follows the collapse, and, for the runs of Section 5, a front-following axial map described there. It reproduces Hou's published checkpoints: the Euler vorticity ratio at his first checkpoint to one percent and the Navier-Stokes ratio at t = 0.0022616 to three percent (x144 against his 139.6); the details of the cross-code comparison, including the spread among Hou's own tabulated and fitted values, are in the supplementary record (Rounds 86-87).

### 2.2 Azimuthal-mode solvers

Perturbations of azimuthal wavenumber m about an axisymmetric base are computed in three ways. The frozen-base solver (axi3dlin) linearizes about a snapshot and integrates the mode with an exact discrete pressure projection (the divergence-of-gradient operator assembled from the same difference stencils, banded, with a Neumann wall row so that the projected radial velocity vanishes at the wall) and the parity rules u_r', u_theta' ~ r^{m-1}, u_z', p' ~ r^m at the axis; viscosity enters through the combinations u_r +- i u_theta, which obey the Laplacians of index m +- 1. The tangent-linear solver (axi3dtl) marches the nonlinear axisymmetric base and the mode in one Runge-Kutta step, linearizing about the instantaneous base at every stage, with the base's moving radial map installed as a fixed map and mapped bases transferred by exact sine series. The nonlinear solver (axi3dnl) carries modes m = 1 to M - 1 with the azimuthal convolutions evaluated pseudo-spectrally on 3M points and feeds the m = 0 Reynolds stress back into the base equations.

For m = 0 the seed exponents become u_r', u_theta' ~ r and u_z' ~ 1, and the axial-mean Neumann problem for the pressure is singular and is pinned at the axis.

### 2.3 Gates

Solid-body rotation is neutral to 1e-10 in the frozen solver. A Rayleigh-unstable column reproduces a one-dimensional primitive-variable eigensolver (0.3708 for m = 0; 0.248 + 0.337 i for the physical m = 1 mode, with wall-localized spurious modes identified and excluded). The nonlinear code with a seed of 1e-6 reproduces the tangent-linear rate to three digits (0.158 against 0.160) and generates the m = 2 harmonic at second order. The tangent-linear rate at equal fraction of the remaining time reproduces the frozen-base rate to two percent up to a fifth of the remaining time.

### 2.4 The trivial directions and the threshold

On a Type I base u = (T - t)^{-1/2} U(x / sqrt(T - t)) the amplitude rate sigma of a perturbation, in units of the collapse rate 1/(T - t), is 3/2 for the time-translation direction d_t U and 1 for an axis translation; a perturbation outruns the collapse (its ratio to the base grows) when sigma (T - t) > 1/2. For m = 1 the axis-translation field -d_x U is an exact tangent-linear solution and is projected out with the energy inner product (exact for a normal operator; the overlap stays below 0.3 in all runs, so the projection error is at the ten percent level or below); for m = 0 the overlap with d_t U is monitored and stays below 0.1. We report both the raw rate and the rate of the orthogonal part where it matters.

### 2.5 Certificates

Every long-wave entry of the table has three axial resolutions (512, 1024 and 2048 points, with the localized seed pinned at the same position) and a radial pair (an exact subsample of the sinh-map grid to 257 points against 513). The short-wave families have an axial pair (1024 against 2048) on the viscous base. The viscous continuation has a twin at a different axial map and Courant number, and the front-resolved runs have two maps. Three earlier readings were retracted during the work and are recorded as such in the supplementary log: a fit-dependent 'departure' of the Type I constants (the singular-time degeneracy of Section 5.1), a grid-scale event in a seeded run at the x200 state, and a three-cell front thickness. We regard the retractions as part of the certificate.

## 3. The spectral table

### 3.1 Euler, the x28 state

The base is the Euler solution at t = 0.002208, where the swirl amplitude is 28 times its initial value, the core sits at (R, Z) = (0.0250, 0.0078), and the Type I constant sup |u| (|x| + sqrt(T - t)) is 75 (it is 75 on every state from x28 to x200, set by the outer flow).

*Long-wave m = 1.* On the frozen base the localized seed grows at 0.184, 0.232, 0.275, 0.313, 0.346, 0.375, 0.401, 0.425, 0.448 at 2.2, 4.3, 6.5, 8.7, 10.9, 13.0, 15.2, 17.4, 19.5 percent of the remaining time; at three axial resolutions the rate at 8.7 percent reads 0.318, 0.313, 0.319 and at 13 percent 0.379, 0.375, 0.380 (512, 1024, 2048 points: agreement to 1.3 percent), and the radial subsample reads 0.306 at 8.7 and 0.351 at 11.6 percent (2 percent). On the co-evolving base the rate rises 0.25, 0.34, 0.42, 0.49, 0.56, 0.64, 0.71, 0.76, 0.82 at 6, 11, 17, 23, 29, 34, 40, 45.6, 46.3 percent, thirteen percent above the frozen rate at equal fraction; after projecting out the translation direction the orthogonal part reads 0.40, 0.55, 0.62, 0.67, 0.80 at 23, 30, 38, 44, 47 percent. The non-trivial m = 1 perturbation therefore outruns the Euler collapse from about 30 percent of the remaining time and reaches 0.8 of the collapse rate by 47 percent. Its axial wavenumber centroid co-scales with the collapse (7 to 30 cycles per unit as the core height shrinks), the swirl share of its energy production rises from 0.07 to 0.39, and its precession rate omega (T - t) passes from +0.12 through zero to retrograde near 40 percent; the swirling base has only SO(2) symmetry, so this reversal is permitted and is not a bifurcation signature.

*Long-wave m = 2.* 0.31 at 17 percent on the frozen base, rising.

*Long-wave m = 0.* The localized seed on the frozen base rises to a maximum of 0.495 at 11.7 percent and falls to 0.378 by 29 percent; at three axial resolutions the maximum reads 0.495, 0.496, 0.497 (512, 1024, 2048 points) and the radial pair agrees to 1 percent (0.484 against 0.489 at 8.7 percent, 0.497 against 0.495 at 11.6). On the co-evolving base it plateaus at 0.454 at 10 percent and falls to 0.389 by 27 percent, with the overlap with d_t U at 0.08 to 0.10. The long-wave axisymmetric direction is stable: no non-trivial axisymmetric unstable direction exists at this level, consistent with Hou's report that the profile is 'very stable' to perturbations of the data.

*Short-wave families.* A smooth random seed under the same envelope excites, for every m, a family living in a thin band on the front whose axial wavenumber centroid marches with the collapse (17 to 80 cycles per unit for m = 0; 26 to 54 for m = 1 over the resolved range) and whose production is entirely from the meridional shear. Its rate has no maximum in Euler: for m = 1 it reaches 0.9 of the collapse rate by 16 percent; for m = 0 it crosses 1/2 at 8 percent and reaches 0.72 by 17 to 20 percent before the axial resolution limits it. This is the Kelvin-Helmholtz continuum of the front, in Hou's words the 'shearing instability' that 'typically occurs near the sharp front' and that his numerical viscosity is chosen to suppress [H22a, Sec. 3.6].

### 3.2 Navier-Stokes, the x144 state, viscosity 5e-4

The base is Hou's first-stage solution at t = 0.002262 (amplitude 144 times the initial, his tabulated 139.6), whose amplitude still grows like (T - t)^{-0.85}.

*Long-wave m = 1* (co-evolving): 0.13, 0.19, 0.24, 0.30, 0.37, 0.43, 0.48, 0.50 at 1.5, 6, 10, 16, 23, 29, 36, 38 percent; orthogonal part 0.32, 0.34, 0.39, 0.39 at 24, 32, 37, 40 percent, and 0.44 at 51 percent. Viscosity holds the long-wave mode below the threshold on this base.

*Short-wave families* (co-evolving, random seed): m = 1 reads 0.475, 0.627, 0.768 at 10, 14, 18 percent, with the frozen-base axial pair agreeing to 3 to 7 percent (0.116 against 0.093 at 4 percent, 0.381 against 0.368 at 8); m = 0 crosses 1/2 at 12 percent and reads 0.58 at 20 percent; m = 2 crosses at 9 percent and saturates near 1.13 by 37 percent. The front's continuum is unstable relative to the collapse at every azimuthal wavenumber, with rates within about twenty percent of one another: it is a property of the sheet, not of a symmetry class. The sheet Reynolds number Delta U / sqrt(nu a) with the measured velocity jump (Section 5) is 120 to 150 and, under the Type I scaling, constant in time, so the family is never frozen out.

### 3.3 Navier-Stokes, the x144 state, viscosity 5e-3

Hou raises the viscosity tenfold at t0 = 0.00227375 because 5e-4 'is not strong enough to stabilize the shearing instability generated by the 3D Euler equations' [H23b, Sec. 3]. Applying 5e-3 to base and mode from the x144 state, a random seed first decays (rates -1.3 in collapse units at one percent) and its surviving part re-grows: m = 1 crosses zero at 14 percent and 1/2 at 27 percent and reads 0.74 at 37 percent; m = 0 crosses zero at 19 percent and 1/2 at 37 percent; the long-wave m = 1 direction rises slowly to 0.46 at 36 percent and stays below the threshold. The second-stage viscosity delays the axisymmetric family from 12 to 37 percent and the non-axisymmetric one from about 11 to 27 percent; it removes neither. The base at 5e-3 lags Type I from the start (amplitude 1.048 against 1.064 at 6 percent).

### 3.4 The crossing fraction versus viscosity

For the m = 1 random-seed family on the co-evolving x144 base, the fraction of the remaining time at which the rate crosses 1/2 is 11 percent at 5e-4, 11.5 at 1e-3, 17.4 at 2e-3, 27 at 5e-3 and 30.2 at 1e-2 (the last run starting at -2.6 and crossing zero at 20.5 percent). Over the decade from 1e-3 to 1e-2 the crossing fraction rises by nineteen points, roughly linearly in the logarithm of the viscosity and flattening at the top. At every viscosity from Hou's first stage to twice his second, the non-axisymmetric family outruns the collapse before a third of the remaining time has elapsed; extrapolating the trend, holding it below the collapse rate to half the remaining time would need a viscosity of order 3e-2, at which the base no longer collapses like Type I.

### 3.5 An analytic cross-check

Leibovich and Stewartson's sufficient condition for non-axisymmetric short-wave instability of a swirling flow, 2 V Omega [D(rV) D Omega + D(W^2)] < 0 with V the swirl, Omega = V / r, W the axial velocity and D the radial derivative, is evaluable on the axisymmetric base alone. It is satisfied in a thin annulus just outside the swirl maximum on every state examined: at (r, z) = (0.0273, 0.0078) against the maximum at (0.0250, 0.0078) on the Euler x28 state, at (0.0141, 0.0037) against (0.0138, 0.0037) on the viscous x144 state, and at (0.0030, 0.0009) against (0.0029, 0.0008) at x222; the implied short-wave growth is of order the collapse rate. Rayleigh's centrifugal discriminant is negative on 6, 12 and 33 percent of the column through the swirl maximum inside twice its radius on the three states.

The evaluation at the viscous x144 state is made rigorous as a statement about an explicit field. Let Omega(s) and W(s) be the degree-20 Chebyshev polynomials in s = (r / r_max)^2, r_max = 2R, with exactly rational coefficients (the double-precision least-squares fit to the column through the swirl maximum, which reproduces the grid column to 1.9 percent in the swirl and 4.5 percent in the axial velocity), and let (V, W) = (r Omega, W) be the columnar vortex they define. Then Phi = 2 r Omega^2 B(r) with B a polynomial of degree 80 with rational coefficients, and by Sturm's theorem in exact rational arithmetic B has no real root in [0.013898, 0.015068] while B is negative at the midpoint; hence Phi < 0 on 1.009 R <= r <= 1.094 R. The explicit columnar field therefore satisfies Leibovich and Stewartson's sufficient condition for non-axisymmetric short-wave instability on that annulus. (The computation is in the accompanying script; an interval-arithmetic evaluation of the Chebyshev series failed by the dependency problem at this degree, which is why the exact polynomial route is used. The same certificate on the Euler x28 column, degree 30 on [0, 1.5 R], holds on 0.998 R <= r <= 1.197 R, but there the polynomial reproduces the grid column only to 16 percent in the swirl because of the sharper inviscid front; we report it as supplementary.)

### 3.6 The columnar model of the front and a certified eigenvalue

*The columnar model.* A column of the flow at fixed height, treated as a parallel swirling flow (V, W) = (r Omega, W) with axial velocity, has normal modes exp(i m theta + i k z - i omega t) governed by a one-dimensional eigenvalue problem, which we solve by Chebyshev collocation with the axis conditions imposed by parity and no-slip at the outer edge of the fitted column (2 R). Two cautions attach to the certificate of Section 3.5. First, it is a statement about the explicit polynomial field; that field satisfies the criterion where the grid field does (the grid field's discriminant is negative on the single band 1.00 R to 1.81 R with the same depth, the polynomial's on wiggly sub-segments of it), but the polynomial's first derivative is wrong by up to a factor two at the swirl maximum, and its normal-mode eigenvalues do not represent the flow's: a one-dimensional columnar normal-mode solver (Chebyshev collocation, the column treated as a parallel swirling flow with axial velocity) finds a fast near-front mode on the polynomial field and none on the grid column through the swirl maximum.

*The front column.* Second, the column that carries the instability is the front's, not the swirl maximum's: on the grid column through the front at the height where the two-dimensional m = 1 mode peaks, the columnar solver has an isolated unstable viscous m = 1 eigenvalue with growth 0.81 in collapse units at k R = 28 (converged in resolution and domain, 20 percent above the next eigenvalue, inviscid limit 0.83 to 0.89 and still rising slowly with k), against 0.79 to 0.80 for the frozen-base two-dimensional rate at 20 percent of the remaining time. The columnar rate varies from 0.5 to 1.05 across the front's thickness, the columnar eigenfunction sits at 1.3 to 1.4 R where the two-dimensional mode peaks at 1.0 R, and on the inviscid x28 state, whose front is sharper and whose instability is the axial-shear (Kelvin-Helmholtz) mode of the free front, no column reproduces the two-dimensional rate at all (columnar rates 0.4 to 0.7 against 0.92, at twice the radius); so the parallel-flow model is local, identifies the mechanism on the viscous front and brackets rather than predicts the two-dimensional rate; but it is a discrete, well-separated eigenvalue of a one-dimensional problem, which is the object a rigorous enclosure can reach (Section 7.3).

*Mechanism.* Its mechanism is centrifugal: on the front column the circulation r^2 Omega decreases outward beyond 1.2 R, so Rayleigh's discriminant is negative there (on 1.21 R to 2.0 R), and the inviscid m = 1 rate approaches the local bound sqrt(-Phi_R) = 0.96 collapse units from below as k grows (0.83, 0.87, 0.89 at k R = 22, 44, 88) with the eigenfunction narrowing onto the radius of the bound's maximum, which is the fixed-m large-k limit of Billant and Gallaire [BG05, BG13]; the viscous peak at k R = 28 is the Mayer-Powell cutoff of that branch [MP92]. The axial flow of the collapse extends the centrifugal instability to every azimuthal wavenumber [BG13], which is why the m = 0, 1 and 2 short-wave families of Section 3.2 cross the threshold within a few percent of each other (on the front column the viscous columnar peaks are 0.79, 0.81, 0.80 and 0.76 collapse units for m = 0, 1, 2, 3, at k R = 22, 28, 33 and 44, all in the same annulus: one centrifugal instability of the front at every azimuthal wavenumber, not three); without axial flow a Rayleigh-stable column is spectrally stable [GS20], and no rigorous or computer-assisted result exists for an unstable eigenvalue of a swirling column with axial flow.

*A certified discrete eigenvalue.* We therefore certify the discrete columnar problem. Let Omega(s) and W(s) be the degree-30 polynomials in s = (r / r_max)^2, r_max = 2R, with exactly rational coefficients (the double-precision Chebyshev fit of the front column, converted exactly; the fit reproduces the grid column's eigenvalue to 0.01 percent), and let (A_N, B_N) be the N-point Chebyshev-Gauss collocation pencil of the linearized viscous problem (m = 1, k = 2000, nu = 5e-4, no-slip imposed at the outermost collocation point r_max cos(pi / 4N), axis conditions by parity) on the field (r Omega, W), with every entry enclosed in interval arithmetic. Then, by a Krawczyk verification with rigorous floating-point error bounds (Rump's method; midpoint-radius matrices; all products bounded by the classical gamma_n inequalities; the code and its tests accompany the paper), the pencil has a simple eigenvalue in the disc of radius 1.2e-7 about omega = -3.5051747e4 + 5.0850344e4 i for N = 150, with its eigenvector in an enclosed box in which the eigenpair is unique (the disc is not certified to contain no other eigenvalue; the nearest floating-point eigenvalue is 1.05e4 away), and likewise for N = 100 (radius 5.2e-8, agreeing to nine digits) and N = 60 (5.0850368e4, radius 2.4e-8). An independent assembly of the pencil at 200 bits without the parity trick agrees with every stored entry within its radius, and that assembly reproduces the Cartesian linearized equations on a test field to 1e-34. In collapse units the certified growth rate is 0.812485 (T - t)^{-1} for each discrete problem. The same certificate at N = 100 gives 0.6580 at Hou's second-stage viscosity 5e-3 (k = 1200), 0.8485 for the inviscid problem (k = 2000), and 0.7886 for m = 0 (k = 1600, nu = 5e-4), each with radius below 1.3e-7, so the discrete columnar instability is certified at both viscosities, inviscid, and at two azimuthal wavenumbers. This is a computer-assisted proof about the discretized problem on an explicit field; it certifies neither the continuous columnar operator (that needs defect and inverse-norm bounds of the Nakao-Plum type, and the nine-digit agreement across N is evidence for it, not proof) nor the two-dimensional collapse, whose operator has no isolated eigenvalue to enclose (Section 3.7). Its assumptions are those of every interval computation on BLAS: the real matrix product is a classical inner-product algorithm, overflow is detected, underflow is floored.

### 3.7 The spectrum of the frozen operator

The rates of Sections 3.1-3.4 are growth rates of evolved perturbations, not eigenvalues. A direct eigenvalue computation of the frozen m = 1 operator (Arnoldi iteration on the matrix-free linearization, largest real part) shows why the distinction matters. On the Euler x28 state at 257 x 512 the leading eigenvalues are complex pairs with sigma (T - t) = 3.14 and 3.04 (frequencies +-115 and +-110 in collapse units), residual ||L v - lambda v|| / ||v|| = 7e-6, with axial wavenumber centroid at half the resolved range and radial centroid just inside the swirl maximum: they are the short-wave shearing continuum of Section 7.1 at the grid scale, whose rate grows with resolution, and not the long-wave rate 0.3-0.5 that the localized seed excites. The long-wave direction is therefore not a leading discrete eigenvalue of the inviscid frozen problem, and an inviscid eigenvalue enclosure would be a statement about the grid.

The viscous operator (nu = 5e-4) damps the short waves and should have a resolution-convergent leading eigenvalue; that is the object a rigorous enclosure would target. Plain Arnoldi did not reach it: at 513 x 1024 the iteration failed to converge in 400 restarts, and at 257 x 512 with a Krylov space of 60 it had not returned after 8.5 CPU-hours (about 30,000 operator applications) and was stopped. The leading eigenvalues sit in a cluster whose separation is of the order of the viscous damping of neighbouring short waves, which is the regime in which unshifted Arnoldi stalls [MR96, LS01, T11]; the remedies are a shift-invert or Cayley transform with an inner linear solve, or the exponential (time-stepper) transform of Edwards, Tuckerman, Friesner and Sorensen [ETFS94], Arnoldi on exp(L Delta), which needs no inner solve and contracts the damped continuum to the origin. We tried both matrix-free routes: Arnoldi on the propagator over a tenth of the remaining time (190 applications, eight restart cycles) did not converge, and shift-invert with an inner GMRES solve stalled at a 36 percent inner residual for a real shift at half the threshold, with or without a shifted-diffusion preconditioner, which says the shifted operator is nearly singular over a region: the shift lies inside the pseudospectrum of the damped continuum rather than near an isolated eigenvalue. The leading viscous eigenvalue is therefore not isolated at this resolution, and this is the solver's view of the same continuum that Section 7.1 describes; it also bounds what a rigorous enclosure of the two-dimensional operator could achieve, since every enclosure radius scales with the inverse of the distance to that continuum. The reachable rigorous statement is the one-dimensional radial eigenvalue problem on the front column, certified at the discrete level in Section 3.6. The evolved-perturbation rates of Sections 3.2-3.3, certified by their resolution pairs, stand independently of this.

## 4. Nonlinear fate

The m = 1 mode was seeded at 20 and 50 percent of the local swirl velocity on the Euler x28 state, at 20 percent on the viscous x144 state, and at 20 percent on the Euler x200 state, with m = 0, 1, 2 (and 3) carried and the Reynolds-stress feedback on the base. Against the unperturbed collapse at matched times the axisymmetric amplitude lags: on Euler x28 by 0.65, 1.3, 2.1, 3 percent at 10, 13, 16, 21 percent of the remaining time with the 20 percent seed and by 5 percent at 11 percent with the 50 percent seed, at which the axisymmetric swirl maximum stalls and falls while the three-dimensional swirl maximum stays flat at 1.9 times the axisymmetric one, the mode's own growth drops below its linear rate (0.26 against 0.31), and the m = 2 harmonic sits on the eps^2 curve at 8e-3 of the m = 1 energy without migrating. The x28 continuation reached 30 percent with a 17 percent lag and the three-dimensional swirl ratio flat at 1.37. On the viscous base the 20 percent localized seed lags by 0.2 percent at 9 percent and by about 7 percent at 24 percent.

A random seed on the viscous x144 base (three modes, seed 0.2, followed to 37 percent of the remaining time) tests whether the short-wave family rolls up into an intensifying substructure of the kind seen in strained mixing layers and tornado subvortices. The three-dimensional vorticity peak rose from 1.04 to 1.71 times the axisymmetric peak while the axisymmetric amplitude lagged Type I by 9 percent and the second harmonic stayed at two percent of the first. The end-state fields show the mode's energy density peaking on the ring (radius 0.98 of the swirl maximum's, height 0.91 of its) with half-widths of 0.7 to 0.8 collapse lengths, thirty times the Burgers thickness sqrt(nu (T - t)), and the swirl at that point varying by a factor 1.38 around the azimuth. No compact off-axis maximum at the viscous scale, no Burgers-locked core, no climbing harmonics: the rising vorticity ratio is the linear family growing on a base it depletes.

The outcome is the generic saturating Reynolds-stress feedback of a supercritical mode (Stuart-Watson), the mechanism by which Hou and Li's simulation of Kerr's antiparallel tubes was depleted. A perturbation of relative size eps reaches order one only after an amplification eps^{-1/lambda} with lambda = sigma - 1/2 of order 0.1 to 0.3, which for eps = 1e-2 is 1e10: before the singular time in exact arithmetic, beyond any computation in practice, and depleting once it arrives. We found no seed, state or viscosity at which the symmetry-broken state continued to collapse.

## 5. The viscous continuation

### 5.1 The singular time and the amplitude exponent

Hou's own caveat is that the Type I rate inequalities 'would be almost impossible to verify numerically since it requires an exact value of T*'. We fit T by the log-derivative method: for A ~ C (T - t)^{-p} the ratio A / A' equals (T - t) / p, linear in t with slope -1/p and zero at T, so sliding-window fits give p and T together with nothing assumed, and Luo and Hou's criterion is that both stop moving as the window slides toward T. On the resolved snapshots of the continuation (Section 5.2) five-point windows ending at 0.002281, 0.002282, 0.002283, 0.002284, 0.0022845, 0.002285 and 0.0022853 give p = 1.49, 1.37, 1.19, 1.04, 1.05, 1.00, 1.06 and T = 0.0022888, 0.0022881, 0.0022872, 0.0022865, 0.0022866, 0.0022864, 0.0022866: both have stopped moving over the last three windows, at p = 1.03 +- 0.03 and T = 0.0022865 +- 1e-7. The earlier apparent p ~ 1.8 of a fixed-T fit was the concave A / A' of subleading corrections in the early windows. A first reading of 'falling Type I constants' from a 1/A fit with a drifting T was retracted on this basis.

### 5.2 The continuation and the front-following map

Hou's first-stage solution was continued at nu = 5e-4 past his switch time on a 513 x 1024 grid with a moving radial map and an axial map concentrated at the symmetry plane, from 25 to 217 times the initial amplitude, with a twin at a different axial map and Courant number agreeing to 0.5 percent in amplitude through x217; past x217 the front's width fell to two cells and the twins diverged by 27 percent, and the run was stopped. A 2048-point restart from x131 with a three-times finer axial map read the front at three cells at x268, still grid-limited. The solver was then given a front-following axial map: a Gaussian point density centred on the vorticity maximum with a fifth of the points within three front widths, re-fitted with the radial rezone every hundred steps, the fields moved by exact sine-series evaluation; a map contrast above about 500 breaks the mapped Poisson solve, so the window is kept at three front widths (contrast about 140, symmetrization error 2e-5), at which the restart reproduces the state to a percent.

On that map the same x222 state has a front 4.8e-5 wide over ten cells where the coarse map had read 7.7e-5 over three, and after one interval (x268) the front is 3.3e-5 wide over nine cells. The front's full width at half maximum is 0.86, 0.65, 0.62, 0.39, 0.29 collapse lengths sqrt(T - t) and 3.9, 2.9, 2.8, 1.7, 1.3 Burgers lengths sqrt(nu (T - t)) from x131 to x268 (the first three grid-limited), and 1.0 at x331 (six cells). A Gaussian fit of the azimuthal vorticity across the front at x268 (24 cells inside three standard deviations) gives a standard deviation of 0.58 sqrt(nu (T - t)), a relative residual of 5 percent and a baseline of 9 percent of the peak; the strain implied by the thickness, nu (T - t) over the variance, is 2.9 against the measured 2.2 to 3.3; the velocity jump implied by the fit, sqrt(2 pi) delta omega_peak = 2600, agrees with the measured jump of u_r across the layer (2980) and with the integral of the vorticity (3240). ## 6. The scenario against the theorems

### 6.0 The T-free measurement that decides the class

The Liouville theorem of Koch, Nadirashvili, Seregin and Sverak for bounded ancient axisymmetric solutions with swirl (their Theorem 5.3) has one hypothesis beyond boundedness: |v(x, t)| <= C / |x'| for all t < 0, with |x'| the distance to the axis. Under the Leray rescaling of a solution obeying |u(x, t)| <= C_U0 / (|x - x0| + sqrt(T - t)), the rescaled family obeys |u^(lambda)| <= C_U0 / |y'| uniformly, so any ancient limit satisfies the hypothesis with the same constant and is trivial, which contradicts the normalization of the rescaling; the scenario is then regular. The quantity to measure is therefore sup |u| |x'| over the domain as a function of amplification, and unlike every velocity constant of Section 5.3 it does not involve the fitted singular time at all. On the resolved snapshots it reads 72.3, 72.4, 72.2, 72.3, 72.3, 72.3, 72.2, 72.2, 72.2, 72.2, 72.2, 72.1 from 144 to 331 times the initial amplitude, constant to 0.3 percent while log(1/(T - t)) rises from 10.6 to 13.9; the value at the swirl maximum itself is 21 to 22; the Type I constant sup |u| (|x| + sqrt(T - t)) is 75.4 to 74.6; and the circulation maximum is 64.9 to 64.7 by its maximum principle. Hou's stated escape from the exclusion theorems is a logarithmic growth of |u| r, which over this range would read about 30 percent. It does not occur. The computed scenario therefore satisfies, as measured and T-independently, the hypothesis of a published Liouville theorem, and is in the excluded class.

### 6.1 The hypotheses of the exclusion theorems

Chen, Strain, Tsai and Yau [CSTY09, II] prove that axisymmetric strong solutions with swirl in R^3 satisfying |v| <= C_* |t|^{-1/2}, with C_* arbitrary, are regular; no bound of the form |u| <= C / r and no bound on r u^r appears among the hypotheses. Seregin and Sverak [SS09], Theorem 1.1, prove the local statement: for an axisymmetric weak solution in L_3 with pressure in L_{3/2}, a Type I bound on the meridional velocity alone on a parabolic cylinder about an axis point gives regularity there; the hypothesis |v| <= C / |x'| appears only in their Theorem 3.2; and their Lemma 3.3 places singular points on the axis. Hou's gloss that the theorems apply 'provided that |r u^r| and |r u^z| remain bounded' [H24, Sec. 1.5], and his identification of the logarithmic growth of |r u^r| and of the log-weighted circulation as the scenario's escape, therefore do not touch the hypotheses that are actually used; Barker [B25] makes the same observation in R^3 and flags the wall at r = 1 as the point to check, which the locality of the Seregin-Sverak theorem settles. The measured meridional constant of Section 5.3 is bounded. The only escape the theorems leave is a velocity that is not asymptotically Type I, where the Liouville theorem for bounded ancient axisymmetric solutions with swirl is open [KNSS09, AB19]; the sharpest log-supercritical criterion with swirl, Seregin's, allows growth only like (ln ln 1/(T - t))^{1/224}. Our data cannot read the swirl constant's growth law to that precision (Section 5.3), so this escape is neither established nor excluded; every resolved observable, the amplitude exponent, the meridional constant, the front at the Burgers scale, is that of a Type I collapse.

### 6.2 The exact local solution and where finite energy acts

The layer of Section 5.4 is an exact Navier-Stokes solution for any strain history a(t) (Rott 1958; the Lundgren transformation reduces it to the heat equation), and for a = 1 / (T - t) its thickness is sqrt(nu (T - t)) exactly. It belongs to the stretched-vortex class of Gibbon, Fokas and Doering, in which finite-time singularities exist (Ohkitani-Gibbon; Constantin's infinite family of exact Euler solutions of infinite energy; Maekawa, Miura and Prange's backward self-similar Burgers-vortex blowup for Navier-Stokes with linear strain). These escape every theorem by having infinite energy: the KNSS Liouville theorem needs bounded ancient solutions, and the layer rescales to a linearly growing one; the Seregin-Sverak argument needs the rescaled limit to be a suitable weak solution; the Leray-profile theorems of Necas, Ruzicka and Sverak and of Tsai need a profile in L^3 or with local energy control. Finite energy acts at a computable distance: a linear strain 1 / (T - t) gives velocity L / (T - t) at distance L, while a Type I bound limits it to M / sqrt(T - t), so the linear profile survives only to L ~ M sqrt(T - t), about four ring radii, inside the computational domain. The depletion is visible in the numbers: the free exact layer has circulation growing like T / (T - t) and a Type II velocity, ours has a Type I velocity and p = 1, so the layer's circulation exponent is halved by the finite-energy feedback. A core-resolving computation can never falsify the local model, which is exact; it can falsify the global one, and what it shows is a Type I collapse with the front at the viscous scale and no departure to 331 times the initial amplitude.

### 6.3 What the local L^3 norm does and does not say

The decrease of Section 5.5 contradicts no theorem as stated. The global conditions (a singularity requires the whole-space L^3 norm to blow up [ESS03, S12]) say nothing about shrinking balls; Barker and Prange's logarithmic lower bound concerns balls of radius (T - t)^{(1 - delta) / 2} with a doubly exponentially small constant; the at-scale theorems (Barker-Prange 2020, Theorem 2; Kang, Miura and Tsai) are lower bounds by a small universal constant, and the measured 22, which is 4.4e4 in the dimensionless units of the theorems, satisfies them by a wide margin. A Type I profile predicts the scaled norm to approach a constant, and a mild decrease toward a plateau is consistent with that. The sentence the data supports is that the critical norm's content at the collapse scale is not growing, the opposite of the trend a singularity drives into the global critical norm: evidence against, not proof.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 The continuum obstruction

Every rigorous construction of a log-corrected (Type II) singularity in a parabolic or dispersive problem follows one recipe: a stationary or self-similar profile of a limiting problem, its linearized spectrum with finitely many unstable directions and a resonant mode, and a modulation argument. For Navier-Stokes the limiting problem is Euler, viscosity being exactly marginal under the Leray scaling, and this framing exists only as an exclusion program (Seregin's Type II papers, Barker and Prange), never as a construction. Our table supplies the missing spectral ingredient for the leading profile and finds that it fails: the inviscid profile has no non-trivial long-wave unstable direction but a continuum of unstable short-wave directions at every azimuthal wavenumber, whose in-frame rate exceeds the collapse rate from 8 percent of the remaining time. This is the essential spectrum of Lifschitz and Hameiri, Friedlander and Vishik, and Vishik (whose radius equals the maximal Lyapunov exponent of the profile flow), which Chen and Hou reconcile with their stable boundary profile by working in a low-regularity weighted norm where the outgoing transport damps short waves, declining the high-regularity estimate whose coercivity they call unclear. A construction on the interior profile must either find such a norm or cut the continuum off with a viscosity large enough, which is what Hou's tenfold viscosity does by hand for the axisymmetric family and, by Section 3.4, cannot do for the non-axisymmetric one without stopping the collapse. The object that results is a viscously selected profile whose inviscid limit is not an attractor, and an exactly self-similar viscous profile is what the Leray-profile theorems forbid.

### 7.2 The Liouville problem and the log escape

Whether a conditional Liouville theorem could close the log escape was examined directly. The KNSS argument uses |v| <= C / |x'| at two lines, both as a statement uniform over rescalings, and a logarithmic weakening breaks both structurally for every exponent, not by a constant; the published growth-side results reach only a double logarithm to a small power (Pan's exponent 0.028; Seregin's 1/224 in scaled norms), and the counterexample of Seregin, Silvestre, Sverak and Zlatos for drift-diffusion Liouville theorems sits at the next rung. The one hypothesis type that would enter the proof at the level of the source term, a one-sided monotonicity of Gamma^2 in the axial direction, is not satisfied by the computed profile: on the collapse side of the core the sign of d_z(Gamma^2) is mixed (58 to 69 percent negative by weight). The escape reduces to a single unextracted number, the dependence of the axis Holder exponent in the critical-drift oscillation decay on the drift constant; polynomial dependence would close every logarithmic power below a threshold, exponential dependence none. We leave it as the one analytical question this scenario poses.

### 7.3 What remains open

Discretely self-similar and aperiodic (chaotic) Type I singularities are excluded only near the continuous case (Chae and Wolf) and are equivalent, in the aperiodic case, to the Liouville problem for bounded ancient solutions, which is open in three dimensions; but a discretely self-similar singularity is Type I with a point singularity, and in the axisymmetric swirl class every Type I singularity is excluded, so this loophole lives in the non-axisymmetric class, where the relevant computation, a period-log(lambda) or aperiodic orbit of the rescaled equations in three dimensions, has never been attempted for a blowup problem (the closest precedent is Gundlach's computation of the Choptuik spacetime as a periodic orbit in similarity time). The octahedral Kida-Pelz class is unexcluded by any theorem but its Euler collapse was superseded by depletion at higher resolution, and no viscous arrest study of it exists.

On the rigorous side of our own results, Section 3.6 certifies the discrete columnar eigenvalue; the step from the discrete to the continuous columnar operator is the Nakao-Plum programme (a defect bound for the certified eigenfunction and a bound on the inverse of the shifted operator on its complement), which has been carried out for Orr-Sommerfeld and Rayleigh-Benard problems at this size and would make the result the first rigorous unstable eigenvalue of a swirling column with axial flow. Neither step certifies the two-dimensional collapse, whose operator has no isolated eigenvalue to enclose (Section 3.7).

### 7.4 The regularity side

No finite list of computations certifies global regularity for all data, but certification for one datum is a finite computation in principle (Chernyshenko, Constantin, Robinson and Titi; Dashti and Robinson), discharged in practice only for mild data; for Hou's datum the exponential tolerance collapses under a 1e7 amplification. What this work contributes to that side is characterization: a finite-codimension picture in which the collapse is Type I with a converged singular time, the front sits at the viscous scale, the axisymmetric long-wave direction is stable, and the short-wave families, axisymmetric and not, outrun the collapse at both of Hou's viscosities and deplete it when they arrive.

### 7.5 Predictions for a three-dimensional computation

No non-axisymmetric computation of Hou's data exists. The first one will test the following statements, which are the content of this paper reduced to numbers that a single run at nu = 5e-4, started from the axisymmetric solution at 144 times the initial amplitude with a small random perturbation, can confirm or refute.

1. The azimuthal wavenumber m = 1 energy in a thin band on the front grows, in the collapsing frame, faster than the collapse from 11 percent of the remaining time (sigma (T - t) = 0.63 at 14 percent and 0.77 at 18 percent against the threshold 1/2), with m = 0 and m = 2 following at 12 and 9 percent. The long-wave m = 1 direction (a displacement of the collapse) stays below threshold throughout (0.44 at 51 percent).
2. The growth saturates as depletion, not as a sub-vortex: the three-dimensional vorticity maximum reaches 1.7 times the axisymmetric one while the axisymmetric amplitude falls 9 percent behind the Type I law (20 percent seed at 37 percent of the remaining time); the second harmonic stays at about two percent of the first; the mode's energy peaks on the ring at 0.98 of the swirl maximum's radius and 0.9 of its height with half-widths of 0.7 to 0.8 collapse lengths; no compact off-axis maximum forms at the viscous scale.
3. For a seed of relative size eps the symmetry breaking becomes order one after an amplification of eps^{-1/lambda} with lambda between 0.1 and 0.3 beyond the crossing, so a 1 percent seed reaches order one between 200 and 10^6 times the amplitude at which it was introduced; the collapse does not continue through it.
4. At Hou's second-stage viscosity 5e-3 the same instability crosses at 27 percent of the remaining time (0.74 at 37 percent); the tenfold viscosity moves the crossing, it does not remove it.
5. Any resolved axisymmetric continuation of the nu = 5e-4 solution gives sup_x |u| |x'| = 72 +- 1 with no trend over the amplification range 144 to 331, the circulation maximum 64.7 to 64.9, the meridional Type I constant 6 to 8, an amplitude exponent 1.03 +- 0.03 against a converged singular time 0.0022865 +- 1e-7, a front of thickness 1.3 to 1.7 sqrt(nu (T - t)) fitting the Rott-Lundgren profile to 5 percent, and omega (T - t) levelling near 118.

A three-dimensional run in which the symmetry-broken state continues to collapse with its amplitude on the Type I law, or an axisymmetric continuation in which sup |u| |x'| grows logarithmically (about thirty percent over the range above), would refute the corresponding statement and with it the paper's reading of the scenario.
